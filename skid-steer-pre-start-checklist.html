Skid steer pre-start checklist

Use this skid steer pre-start checklist before each shift. It covers the walk-around, the cab and restraint bar, and a short operating test, with a few checks that are specific to skid steers.

This is a general guide only. Always follow the manufacturer's operator manual, your site rules and the workplace health and safety laws that apply to you.

1. Walk-around (engine off)

  • Logbook and plates: serial number matches and safety decals are legible
  • Tyres or tracks: condition, pressure, cuts, damage and wheel nuts
  • Engine oil, hydraulic oil and coolant levels
  • Fuel level, and no fuel or oil leaks under the machine
  • Hoses and fittings: no chafing, cracks or weeping
  • Lift arms, pivot pins and bushes: no cracks or excessive play
  • Bucket or attachment: cutting edge, welds and condition
  • Quick-attach locks: engaged and pinned
  • Cab frame and guarding intact; no cracked or missing panels
  • Battery secure; grease points greased

2. Cab and controls

  • Seat belt fitted and working
  • Lap bar or restraint bar moves freely and latches
  • Door, screen or cab mesh secure; mirrors and camera clean
  • Controls move freely and return to neutral
  • Horn, lights, beacon and reversing alarm working
  • Gauges and warning lights show normal after start-up
  • Fire extinguisher and first aid kit present and in date

3. Operating test

  • Lap bar interlock: with the bar raised, the lift and tilt functions lock out
  • Lift and tilt through full range; check for smooth movement and odd noises
  • Drive forward and reverse; check for pull to one side
  • Parking brake holds on a slight slope
  • Test any attachment and its hydraulic couplers
  • Before work: check ground conditions and the location of overhead and underground services

Frequently asked questions

What should a skid steer pre-start check include?

It should include the walk-around (tyres or tracks, fluids, leaks, lift arms and attachments), the cab and restraint bar, and a short operating test of the lift, tilt, drive and brake functions.

Why check the lap bar on a skid steer?

On most skid steers the lap bar locks out the lift and tilt functions when it is raised. Checking that it latches and that the lockout works is part of a normal pre-start check. Follow the manufacturer's manual for your machine.
